Privacy

What Mast keeps.

Last updated 17 October 2026

Mast locks your phone and computer at the times you set. To do that it keeps a few things. This page says what, where, and who sees them.

On your devices only

  • The apps you block. On iPhone you pick them in Apple’s Screen Time, and Mast never learns which apps they are. On Android the list stays on the phone.
  • Time in apps on your computer: which app is in front and for how long, kept up to 90 days. Mast also reads window titles there, to spot sites like YouTube during a to-do and to notice when a friend’s answer page is open on your own computer. None of this is sent.
  • Your place, if you set one that lets you out of a lock. Your location is checked only when you save it or ask to be let out there.
  • The masjids near you, if you open the map, and the one you chose.
  • Your Google sign-in, if you connect Google Calendar, encrypted by the computer’s operating system.
  • The content filter, a founder feature on the computer. While Instagram, X, Reddit or TikTok is in front, Mast looks at a picture of the window, on the computer, for nudity and faces. The pictures are not saved or sent. It keeps only the gender you gave it and which sites it has blocked, and until when.

In your Mast account

You sign in with your email and a code. There is no password. Your account lets your phone and computer lock together, so Mast keeps these on its servers:

  • Your email address.
  • Your devices: a name for each (on a computer, the computer’s own name), phone or computer, the platform, and when it was last seen.
  • Your locks: prayer, bedtime and calendar settings, changes waiting their 24 hours, and the ways out you allow. If you pause prayer locks for a period, when the pause began.
  • Timed locks you start: when each starts and ends, and how it ended.
  • Your calendars: the private address of each calendar you add, the Google account you connect, and the titles and times of events in the next two days.
  • Where you are, if you allow location on your phone: the latitude, longitude and city used for prayer times, so your computer uses the same times.
  • Your friends: the email, name and phone number you add for each.
  • Asking to be let out: which lock, when it ends, any note you write, how you were let out (a friend, your place, or an emergency), each friend’s answer, and when.
  • Going around a lock: Screen Time access turned off, a lock your computer missed, or an emergency used.
  • Your networks: the internet addresses your devices connect from, kept 30 days, so a friend’s yes can’t come from your own Wi-Fi.
  • Your to-dos, a founder feature: each title, day, time and length, and when it was done.
  • A recitation shared between devices: what is playing and where. It is replaced each time.

Each account can read only its own records. One exception: anyone who has your account’s ID can ask whether a single lock was let out, and get a yes or no. Your computer uses this to keep bedtime without signing in.

We don’t sell any of this, use it for ads, or use it for anything but running Mast.

What your friends see

When you ask to be let out, Mast emails your friends. The email has your email address, the lock’s name and when it ends. You can also text your friends a link from your phone.

A friend signs in on kushu.space with a code texted to the phone number you added for them, and says yes with Face ID or a fingerprint on that phone. Mast keeps a passkey for it (a public key, not their face or fingerprint), and texts them after each yes. They see your email address, the lock’s name, when it ends, any note you wrote, and how many friends have said yes.

Your friends are also emailed if Screen Time access is turned off, if your computer misses a lock, or if you use your weekly emergency.

Your location

Prayer locks need prayer times. If you allow location, your phone’s position is used. If not, Mast guesses your city from your internet address. Either way the latitude and longitude are sent to AlAdhan for the times.

The map of nearby masjids sends your position to OpenStreetMap’s services to find them and the walking route.

Your calendar

If you connect Google Calendar, Mast asks Google for read-only access to your calendar list and your events. It reads the titles and times of the next two days’ events, to lock for the ones you chose. It never changes, creates or deletes anything in your calendar.

The Google sign-in stays on the computer where you made it. The events are kept in your account so your other device can lock for them too. They are replaced every few minutes and removed when you disconnect the calendar or delete your account.

Mast’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Calendar data is used only to run your locks: it is never sold, never used for advertising, and never shown to anyone else.

If you add a calendar by its private address instead, the same applies to the events read from that address.

Services Mast uses

  • Supabase hosts your account and sends your sign-in code.
  • Resend sends the emails to your friend.
  • Cloudflare. On Windows, once you allow it, the adult-site filter makes the computer look up web addresses through Cloudflare for Families, so Cloudflare sees the names of sites the computer visits. It also turns on SafeSearch in your browsers.
  • Google, if you connect Google Calendar.
  • Location. ipwho.is, GeoJS or ipapi.co, to guess your city from your internet address. AlAdhan, for prayer times. OpenStreetMap (Overpass and its routing service) and OpenFreeMap, for the map.
  • The Qur’an and the adhan. Text, fonts, recitations and adhan sounds load from alquran.cloud, Quran.com, QuranicAudio, EveryAyah, GitHub and IslamCan. These requests carry nothing about you.
  • Updates. The phone app checks Expo for updates; test builds come through Apple’s TestFlight. The computer app checks GitHub.

Every service Mast connects to sees your internet address, as any website does. Mast has no ads, analytics or tracking tools.

Deleting your data

To delete your account and everything kept with it, open Settings → Account → Delete account in the Mast app on your phone. It is deleted at once and can’t be undone. You can also email hello@kushu.space from the address you signed in with, and we will delete it. Deleting Mast from a device removes what it kept there.

If you are someone’s friend, your email stays in their list until they remove you.

Children

Mast accounts are not meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly keep their data.

Changes and questions

If this changes, the date at the top will too. Questions are welcome at hello@kushu.space.
